Hometown Kart - Bulli: privacy policy

Last updated: 28 October 2026 (version 1.4.1: gameplay analytics with Flowdar, on Android, iPhone and iPad; the iPhone and iPad version) · Developer: Hometown Kart · Contact: phillipbaden@gmail.com

The short version

  • Playing single player or exploring Bulli needs no account. The game asks for Bulli's weather (see "Live weather") and, when something goes wrong, sends a crash report (see "Crash and error reports"; you can switch them off).
  • Playing online, you sign in with an email and password, a Google account, or (on iPhone and iPad) Sign in with Apple. New guest accounts are no longer offered; a guest account made with an earlier version keeps working and can add a sign-in. The game keeps your racer name, your kart's look and your race times, so other players can race you and see the leaderboard.
  • Friends: you can add friends by racer name. Your friends see your racer name, whether you're online, and the name of the race you're in (you can appear offline). You can invite friends to your race; invites reach them in the game or, if they allow it, as a notification on their phone.
  • Accounts, friends and results are held by Supabase, the account and database service the game uses, in its Sydney region. Notifications are delivered by Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging on Android and Apple's Push Notification service on iPhone and iPad.
  • From version 1.3.2 on Android, the game tells Meta (the company behind Facebook and Instagram) when it is installed and opened, so we can see which of our Facebook and Instagram ads bring new players. See "Measuring our ads (Android)".
  • From version 1.4.1 on Android, iPhone and iPad, the game sends gameplay analytics to Flowdar, the analytics service we use: which menus you open and how your races go (your place, times, crashes and where on the track they happened), so we can make the game more fun. Never your racer name, email address, or anything you type. See "Analytics".
  • No ads in the game, no location from your device, no contacts, and no data sold.

What is stored on your device

Your settings (graphics, sound, frame rate, controls, notification choices), your racer name and your kart (driver, body and three colours). If you have signed in for online play, also your sign-in session (a login token and a refresh token, and the email address of an email account) so you stay signed in. A random ID for this installation of the game (made on first launch; not linked to the device's hardware) and, on a phone or tablet with notifications, the device's notification token. While crash reports are on, also the last lines of the game's log and any reports waiting to be sent (ten at most). On Android (from version 1.3.2), Meta's measurement software (see below) keeps its own random ID for this installation and when the game was last opened. On Android, iPhone and iPad (from version 1.4.1), Flowdar's analytics software (see "Analytics") keeps its own random ID for this installation and the analytics events waiting to be sent (at most 1,000, for at most 7 days). Signing out removes the session; switching crash reports off removes the reports and the log lines; uninstalling the game removes everything.

Live weather

When Weather is set to Live (the default), the game shows Bulli's real weather. To do that it asks the Norwegian Meteorological Institute's free weather service (api.met.no) for the current forecast for Bulli: the request names Bulli's fixed coordinates and the game's name, never your location or anything about you, and it is made when the game starts and every half hour while it runs. Like any internet request it comes from your device's internet address, which the weather service receives under its own privacy terms (met.no). The answer is kept on your device for a few hours. Set Weather to any other choice in Settings and the game makes no requests at all.

Crash and error reports

When the game hits an error or closes unexpectedly, it makes a short report so the problem can be found and fixed, and sends it to the game server the next time it can reach it (no account needed). A report holds: what went wrong, the last lines of the game's own log before it (email addresses, account and race IDs, internet addresses and your racer name taken out), the game's version, your device model, operating system, graphics chip and memory, the graphics setting and how long the game had been running. A report carries no account ID, so it is not linked to you. Reports are kept for 90 days, then deleted automatically. Settings › Crash reports switches them off.

Measuring our ads (Android)

We advertise Hometown Kart on Facebook and Instagram. To see which ads lead to people installing the game, the Android version (from 1.3.2) includes Meta's software development kit (the "Meta SDK", only its core part for counting app events: no Facebook login, no sharing, no ads). Each time the game starts, the Meta SDK sends Meta:

  • that the game was installed (the first time) or opened, and how long you played (start and end of the session);
  • the game's ID at Meta, and the Google Play install referrer (which says, for an install that came from one of our ads, which ad it was);
  • your device's advertising ID (unless you have deleted it in Android's settings), a random ID for this installation, information about the device such as its model, Android version, language, time zone, screen size and mobile network, and the internet address the request comes from.

It does not send your racer name, account, email address, race results, friends, invites or anything you do in a race. Meta uses this to report to us how many installs our ads brought, and uses and keeps it under its own terms and privacy policy (facebook.com/privacy/policy), which can include improving its ads. Your choices are under "Your choices". The iPhone, iPad, PC and Xbox versions don't include the Meta SDK.

Analytics

We use the information described in this policy, such as race results, crash reports and how many times each kind of invite link is opened, to understand how the game is played and to make it better.

From version 1.4.1, the Android, iPhone and iPad versions also send gameplay analytics to Flowdar (flowdar.ai), the analytics service we use, which processes them for us. We use them to see what players enjoy and where they get stuck (for example which parts of a track wreck the most karts, or whether new players finish their first race), so we can improve the game, and to see which ads and invite links bring players who keep playing. The game sends Flowdar:

  • What happens in the game: the menu pages you open; the track you pick; settings you change (for example sound or steering assist); for each race: when it starts and ends, your place and times, lap times, how you drove (for example drift boosts and items used), what you hit and how often (walls, traffic, other karts, kangaroos, magpies, items), where on the track you crashed or were put back on the track and why, whether you finished, restarted or quit, and the game's frame rate; for online play: Quick race, joining or making a race room and how (for example by an invite or a code), and your online results; when you tap a share button and which app you picked to share with (never who you sent it to or the message); how many in-game invites you sent; that you added a friend (not who); how you signed in (guest, Google, Apple or email); the answer you gave to the notifications question; your answer to the tracking question on iPhone and iPad (on Android, whether the advertising ID is on); and problems such as a failed connection.
  • About the game and device: the game's version, the device's model and Android or iOS version, its language and time zone, a random ID for this installation (on iPhone and iPad, the identifier Apple gives our apps on that device), the session (when you open and leave the game), and the country (and possibly region) Flowdar works out from the internet address the data comes from.
  • Where you came from: on Android, the Google Play install referrer (which link, ad or store page led to the install) and Meta's install referrer for an install from one of our Facebook or Instagram ads; on iPhone and iPad, whether the game came from the App Store or TestFlight, and Apple's answer to whether an Apple Search Ads ad led to the install (and if so which campaign, ad group and keyword: Apple works this out without tracking you); and on all of them, the links that open the game (with the tags we put on our own links, such as which share button they came from).
  • Your advertising ID on Android, unless you have deleted it or limited ad tracking in Android's settings. Flowdar keeps it apart from the analytics; it is there so we can later tell ad networks which of our ads brought players who kept playing. On iPhone and iPad the game asks Apple's question "Allow Hometown Kart to track your activity across other companies' apps and websites?" when its main menu first appears: only if you tap Allow does it send Flowdar Apple's advertising identifier, for the same purpose. Whatever you answer, the game works the same, and you can change your answer at any time in your iPhone's Settings.
  • If you play online, your account's random user ID on the game server, so your analytics follow you to a new phone. Never your racer name or email address.

Analytics are not linked to your racer name or email address, are not sold, and are not used to show you ads in the game (there are none). Tests and the PC and Xbox versions send nothing to Flowdar. To ask for your analytics to be deleted, email us (see "Your choices").

Signing in, the game talks directly to Supabase over an encrypted connection (HTTPS); the game server checks your sign-in with Supabase and stores your racer name, results and friends there. If you forget your password, the game asks Supabase to email you a link to a page on hometownkart.bitscience.ai where you choose a new one; the page sends the new password straight to Supabase.

Notifications. Race invites, friend requests and news are sent through Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging (Android) or Apple's Push Notification service (iPhone and iPad), which receive the notification token and the notification's text (for example "Phil invites you to race"). News and events are off until you turn them on in Settings; even then you get at most one every two days and three a week, never at night by your phone's clock. A notification you open is counted (which kind of link, which channel, which campaign; not who). Signing out stops this device getting your account's invites and requests.

Sharing and links. Invite on WhatsApp, Share and Copy the invite link open WhatsApp, your phone's share sheet or the clipboard with a message for you to send: the game never reads your contacts and sends nothing by itself. The link holds the race's code (or your friend link's code) and which way it was shared. When a link opens the game, the game counts its kind and the way it was shared, not who opened it. On Android, if someone installs the game from your link, Google Play passes the link's code to the game once (the install referrer) so it can take them straight to your race.

Server logs (including the addresses) are kept for 30 days, then deleted automatically. Your account, racer name, race results and friends stay until you ask for them to be removed. The connection to the game server is encrypted (TLS), and the game checks it is talking to the real Hometown Kart server before it sends your sign-in.

What the game does not do

  • No ads in the game and no data sold. Data is shared with the services that run the game for us (Supabase for accounts, results and friends; AWS for the game server; Google Firebase Cloud Messaging and Apple's Push Notification service to deliver notifications) and, on Android, with Meta for counting the installs our ads bring (see "Measuring our ads") and, on Android, iPhone and iPad, with Flowdar, which processes the game's analytics for us (see "Analytics").
  • No location from your device (no GPS or location permission), and no camera, microphone, photos or your phone's contacts (your friends in the game are the players you add there).
  • No in-game chat or messages between players: invites name the race and who sent them, nothing else.
  • The game engine (Unity) is set not to send its own usage statistics, and no Unity services are used.

Children

The game is suitable for all ages and is listed on Google Play for ages 13 and over; our ads are shown only to adults. It does not knowingly collect personal information from children. Online play needs a sign-in; racer names and race names are filtered for offensive words, and there is no chat. Friends are added only when both players agree, any player can be blocked, and Friend requests: Nobody in Settings stops new requests.

Your choices

  • You can play everything except online races without an account.
  • Switch off crash reports: Settings › Crash reports.
  • Hide your status from friends: Settings › Friends & notifications › Appear offline. Stop friend requests: Friend requests › Nobody on the same page. Block a player from their page in Friends or a race; unblock in Settings › Friends & notifications › Blocked players.
  • Choose which notifications you get: Settings › Friends & notifications (Race invite alerts, Friend request alerts, News & events), or turn the game's notifications off in your phone's settings.
  • Meta's ad measurement on Android: delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings (Settings › Privacy › Ads › Delete advertising ID on Android 12 and later; the names vary by phone), and Meta can no longer link the game's events to your other activity. If you use Facebook or Instagram, Your activity off Meta technologies in Meta's Accounts Centre lets you see and disconnect what apps send Meta.
  • To delete your account in the game: Settings › Delete account, pressed twice. It deletes your account, racer name, race results, leaderboard places, friends, lists, invites, race history and this account's notification devices straight away, and signs the game out. For an account made with Sign in with Apple, the game asks you to confirm with Apple once more, so the game's access to your Apple Account is withdrawn too (you can also stop using Sign in with Apple with the game in your iPhone's Settings, under your name).
  • Or email phillipbaden@gmail.com with your racer name (and the email address of your account, if it has one); we will delete them within 30 days.
  • Analytics: on Android, deleting your advertising ID (above) stops it being sent; on iPhone and iPad, turning off tracking for the game (Settings › Privacy & Security › Tracking) stops Apple's advertising identifier being sent. To have your analytics deleted, email us with your racer name if you play online (or the approximate date you installed the game); we will delete them within 30 days.

Where the data is kept

Accounts, racer names, results and friends: with Supabase (supabase.com), in its Sydney, Australia region. The game server that runs the races and keeps the crash reports, operated by Hometown Kart: Amazon Web Services (AWS) in Sydney, Australia. Notifications are delivered by Google Firebase Cloud Messaging (firebase.google.com) on Android and by Apple's Push Notification service on iPhone and iPad. The Android install and launch counts go to Meta Platforms (meta.com), on its servers, which are mostly outside Australia. The analytics from Android, iPhone and iPad go to Flowdar (flowdar.ai), on its servers. If more servers are added in other regions, the game will let you choose one, and this policy will list them.
