Hometown Kart - Bulli: privacy policy

Last updated: 11 October 2026 · Developer: Hometown Kart · Contact: phillipbaden@gmail.com

The short version

  • Playing single player or exploring Bulli, nothing leaves your device.
  • Playing online, you sign in as a guest (no details asked) or with an email and password (or, where the game offers it, a Google or Apple account). The game keeps your racer name, your kart's look and your race times, so other players can race you and see the leaderboard.
  • Accounts and results are held by Supabase, the account and database service the game uses, in its Sydney region.
  • No ads, no analytics or tracking, no location, no contacts. No third-party SDKs in the game.

What is stored on your device

Your settings (graphics, sound, frame rate, controls), your racer name and your kart (driver, body and three colours). If you have signed in for online play, also your sign-in session (a login token and a refresh token, and the email address of an email account) so you stay signed in. Nothing here is sent anywhere unless you play online. Signing out removes the session; uninstalling the game removes everything.

Live weather

When Weather is set to Live (the default), the game shows Bulli's real weather. To do that it asks the Norwegian Meteorological Institute's free weather service (api.met.no) for the current forecast for Bulli: the request names Bulli's fixed coordinates and the game's name, never your location or anything about you, and it is made when the game starts and every half hour while it runs. Like any internet request it comes from your device's internet address, which the weather service receives under its own privacy terms (met.no). The answer (the weather in Bulli) is kept on your device for a few hours so the game starts with it. Set Weather to any other choice in Settings and the game makes no requests at all. Without an internet connection the weather is simply sunny. The time of day comes from your device's own clock; nothing is sent for it.

Signing in, the game talks directly to Supabase over an encrypted connection (HTTPS); the game server checks your sign-in with Supabase and stores your racer name and results there. A guest account has no email: if you lose the device or sign out, its results cannot be recovered unless you first add an email to it.

Server logs (including the addresses) are kept for 30 days, then deleted automatically. Your account, racer name and race results stay until you ask for them to be removed. The connection to the game server is encrypted (TLS), and the game checks it is talking to the real Hometown Kart server before it sends your sign-in.

What the game does not do

  • No advertising, no analytics, no tracking across apps or websites, no data sold, and none shared except with the services that run online play (Supabase for accounts and results, AWS for the game server).
  • No location, camera, microphone, contacts or photos.
  • No in-game chat or messages between players.
  • Unity (the game engine): the engine's usage statistics are switched off, and no Unity services (Analytics, Ads, crash reporting) are used.

Children

The game is suitable for all ages. It does not knowingly collect personal information from children: online play works as a guest with no details asked, the racer name is filtered for offensive words, and there is no chat. An email account is optional.

Your choices

  • You can play everything except online races without sending anything.
  • Play online as a guest if you prefer not to give an email address.
  • To delete your account in the game: Settings › Delete account, pressed twice. It deletes your account, racer name, race results and leaderboard places straight away, and signs the game out.
  • Or email phillipbaden@gmail.com with your racer name (and the email address of your account, if it has one); we will delete them within 30 days.

Where the data is kept

Accounts, racer names and results: with Supabase (supabase.com), in its Sydney, Australia region. The game server that runs the races, operated by Hometown Kart: Amazon Web Services (AWS) in Sydney, Australia. If more servers are added in other regions, the game will let you choose one, and this policy will list them.
